Hivemind: Local AI Mesh — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 15 November 2026

Hivemind: Local AI Mesh (“Hivemind”, “the app”) runs AI models on your own devices and lets your phones, tablets and computers work together over your home network. It is made by Kozo Technology, for Android, Windows and Linux. This policy explains what information the app and its advertising partner handle, and the choices you have.

Contact: kozo.technology@gmail.com

The short version

  • The AI runs on your devices. Your questions, the answers, your chats and the files you give the app stay on your devices. We have no server and no accounts, and the app sends none of this to us.
  • On Android the app shows ads from Google AdMob, which collects some device data. In the EU, UK and Switzerland you choose in a consent form, and you can change your choice any time in Settings → Privacy → Privacy options.

Information the app stores on your devices

The app keeps the following on the device where you use it:

  • the AI models you download, and your saved chats, Team jobs and their results
  • files and pictures you attach to a question, while the app works on them
  • your settings, including instructions you write for the models, and, if you set up a cloud model or an API key, that key (stored in the system's secure storage)
  • a key pair that identifies this device to your other devices, and the list of devices you paired (their names, keys and network addresses)
  • statistics about your sessions (speed, time) and about your devices (temperature, battery, memory)

Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, deletes all of this from that device. On Windows and Linux, the app's data folder is described in the user guide on our website; deleting it removes everything. If you turned on Android's device backup, Android may include some of this data in your own Google backup.

Your devices and your home network

When you pair your devices (with a short code or a QR code), they exchange keys and from then on talk only to each other, with every connection encrypted. To answer your questions together, paired devices send each other questions, answers, parts of documents, model files and live status (speed, temperature, battery, memory) over your local network. This traffic stays between your own devices; it never passes through us. To find your other devices, the app announces itself on your local network (mDNS) with the device's name.

On Android 17 and newer, Android asks you to allow access to devices on your network (“Nearby devices”). Hivemind needs it to find, pair and work with your other devices; it does not use it to locate you.

Things that use the internet

  • Downloading models. Models come from Hugging Face (huggingface.co). Like any download, Hugging Face receives your IP address and which files you download. When you add a model from a Hugging Face link, the app asks Hugging Face for that model's file list and sizes. See Hugging Face's privacy policy.
  • A cloud model (optional, Team). If you set up a cloud model with your own API key, the parts of a Team job you send to it go to the provider you chose, under that provider's privacy policy. It is off unless you set it up.
  • Ads and the consent form (Android), described below.
  • The website link. “Send link to computer” opens Android's share sheet; nothing is sent unless you pick an app to share it with.

Once a model is on your device, chatting with it works without internet.

Features you switch on in Android's settings

  • Notification digest and quick replies: if you give Hivemind notification access, it reads your notifications on the device to summarise them with your local model, and sends a reply only when you confirm it. Nothing from your notifications leaves your devices.
  • Digital assistant: if you choose Hivemind as your assistant app, it reads the text on the screen (and, if you allow Android's “Use screenshot”, a screenshot) when you open the assistant, to answer about it on your devices.
  • Voice input and reading aloud use your phone's own speech recognition and text-to-speech services. Depending on your phone and its settings, these Android services may process audio or text online, under their own privacy policies. The app asks for offline recognition where the phone has it.
  • Camera: used only while you scan a pairing QR code. The picture is read on the device by Google's ML Kit, which may send Google anonymous diagnostics about its own performance (no pictures).
  • Connect: other apps on your device can use your models through a local address. If you allow other devices on your network to use it, they need the API key the app shows you.

Advertising and your consent

The Android app shows ads served by Google AdMob: a small native ad at the end of some screens (never in chats, in AI results, in pairing or over other apps), and an optional video you can choose to watch to turn the ads off for 10 hours. AdMob may collect your device's advertising ID, IP address (used for approximate location), device and operating-system information, and ad interaction data to serve and measure ads, and to prevent fraud. See how Google uses this information: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

  • European Economic Area, UK and Switzerland: on first launch the app asks for your consent through Google's certified consent management platform. Personalised ads are shown only if you consent; otherwise you may see limited, non-personalised ads, or no ads.
  • United States: if you live in a US state with a consumer privacy law (such as California), you can opt out of the sale or sharing of your personal information for targeted advertising in Settings → Privacy → Privacy options.
  • Change your choice at any time: you can change or withdraw consent at any time in the app's Settings → Privacy → Privacy options. You can also reset your advertising ID or opt out of ads personalisation in your device's Google settings.

The app works fully whatever you choose. The Windows and Linux apps show no ads and collect nothing for advertising.

Reporting an answer

AI answers are written by the models, which are made by other companies and can be wrong, biased or offensive. Every answer has a Report button. The app shows you the report (your question, the answer, the model's name and your reason) before anything is sent, and sends it to us only when you tap Send. We use reports only to improve the app and its choice of models, and delete them when we no longer need them.

Permissions

On Android the app uses: internet and network state (downloads, ads, consent form); nearby devices / local network, Wi-Fi state and multicast (finding and talking to your own devices; Wi-Fi speed for the dashboard, not location); foreground service, wake lock and notifications (helping your other devices and long AI work with the screen off, and telling you when a job is done); camera (pairing QR codes); “set alarm” (opening the Clock app with a timer or alarm filled in, which you save there). The advertising ID and ad services permissions are added by Google's ads SDK. The app does not ask for your location, contacts, SMS, call log, microphone or files; files reach it only when you pick them.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information, contact us and we will delete it.
